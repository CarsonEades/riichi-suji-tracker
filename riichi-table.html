How the reads work

All of these are rules of thumb for guessing what a player is waiting on. Only genbutsu (a tile that player has already discarded, shown green) is truly safe against them. The rest just make a tile more or less likely to deal in.

Suji

Most riichi hands wait on a two-sided shape (ryanmen), such as 4-5 waiting on 3 or 6. A ryanmen wait always covers two tiles three apart, so the tiles in each suit fall into three lines: 1-4-7, 2-5-8 and 3-6-9.

Furiten means a player can't win by ron on a tile they've discarded. So if they discarded 4, they can't be on a 1-4 or 4-7 ryanmen, and 1 and 7 become suji, safe against two-sided waits.

The middle tiles 4, 5 and 6 sit on two waits each. A 5 is only full suji when both 2 and 8 have been discarded. With just one of them it's half-suji (dark gold).

Suji still loses to closed (kanchan), edge (penchan), shanpon and single-tile (tanki) waits. Honor tiles have no suji.

UUra suji

"Back suji". Ura suji mostly comes from a kanchan (closed wait) that gets upgraded to a ryanmen. Say a player holds the 5-7 kanchan, waiting on 6. They draw an 8, keep the better 7-8 and discard the 5, and now they're waiting on 6-9. The same thing happens the other way: 3-5 plus a drawn 2 becomes 2-3, the 5 goes, and the wait is 1-4.

So a discarded X makes the X+1 / X+4 and X−4 / X−1 lines more dangerous, not less.

Discard 5 → 1, 4, 6, 9. Discard 3 → 4, 7. Discard 7 → 3, 6. Discards of 1, 2, 8 and 9 are ignored, because those tiles are usually thrown as isolated tiles and say little about their neighbors.

MMatagi suji

"Straddling suji": the waits that sit either side of the discarded tile. A player holding 4-5-5 can drop one 5 and keep the 4-5, waiting on 3-6. From 5-5-6 they keep 5-6, waiting on 4-7. Either way the tile they threw sits right inside their wait.

Timing matters. A matagi discard made early in the hand usually makes those tiles safer. Early on, a player tends to cut a middle tile because nothing is connected to it, which points away from that area. A matagi discard made late makes them more dangerous. Close to tenpai, a player cuts a tile from a shape they're finishing, and the riichi declaration tile is the riskiest of all.

Discard 5 → 3, 4, 6, 7. Discard 3 → 1, 2, 4, 5. The badge doesn't track timing, so read it alongside the river, or set U/M from to "riichi tile" to see only the dangerous late case.

AAida yon ken

"Four-gap". It's flagged when a player has discarded two tiles of the same suit five apart, such as 1 and 6. The four tiles between them (2-3-4-5) hide a classic wait: holding 3-4 and cutting the 1 and 6 around it leaves a 2-5 ryanmen.

It's dangerous because neither discard makes 2 or 5 suji, yet the two discards around them make those tiles feel "covered".

1 + 6 → 2, 5. 2 + 7 → 3, 6. 3 + 8 → 4, 7. 4 + 9 → 5, 8.

SSotogawa

"Outside" tiles: those further toward the edge of the suit than a tile the player discarded. A middle tile like 3 connects to more shapes than a 1 or 2. So a player who lets go of a 3 probably isn't building around 1-2-3, which makes the 1 and 2 a bit safer.

Discard 3 → 1, 2. Discard 7 → 8, 9. Discard 6 → 7, 8, 9. A 5 gives no sotogawa. This is the weakest read here: it doesn't block a 2-3 waiting on 1-4, or shanpon and tanki waits.

KKabe

"Wall". When all four copies of a tile are visible, nobody can hold that tile, so no one can have a ryanmen shape that uses it. If all four 7s are out, no one can be waiting on 6-9 with 7-8 or on 5-8 with 6-7. That makes the tiles beyond the wall safer.

A tile gets K when every two-sided shape that could win on it is blocked by a wall. Edge tiles only need one wall: a 7 wall covers 8 and 9, and a 3 wall covers 1 and 2. Middle tiles need a wall on each side: 5 is covered when (3 or 4) and (6 or 7) are both walled.

This is called "no chance". With three copies out ("one chance") a ryanmen is unlikely but still possible, and that isn't marked. Kabe protects against ryanmen waits only, not tanki, shanpon or kanchan. The tracker only counts discards, so tiles in your hand or in other players' calls don't add to a wall, and some real walls won't be marked.

Using the badges

Turn each read on or off in the middle panel. Red, purple and blue badges mark danger. The green S and gray K mark safer tiles. Badges never appear on genbutsu, and they don't change a tile's color.

The U/M from setting picks which discards ura and matagi suji are read from: every discard, or only the riichi tile. The riichi tile carries the strongest signal, since it's the last tile they gave up to reach tenpai.

On your own grid, a danger badge shows if any selected opponent has it. Sotogawa and kabe show only if they hold against all of them.
